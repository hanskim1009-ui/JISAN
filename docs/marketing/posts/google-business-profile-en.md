Visa extension refused in Korea? Check the departure deadline first.

When an extension or change of status is refused, the notice states a departure deadline, in principle within 14 days of the issue date. An administrative appeal or lawsuit can be filed within 90 days, but filing does not stop the departure deadline. To stay in Korea while you challenge the refusal, you need to file together with an application for a stay of execution before the departure deadline.

What to check on the notice:
- The reason for refusal
- The departure deadline, or whether you can stay on your current status

If the reason was missing documents, reapplying may be faster. If it was a judgment call, such as a past fine or doubts about the purpose of your stay, an appeal or lawsuit may be needed.

Called in for police questioning in Korea as a foreign national?

- On the first call, write down the station, team, investigator's name, case number, charge, and whether you are called as a suspect or a witness. You do not need to explain the whole story on the phone.
- You can ask to reschedule, for example to arrange an interpreter or a lawyer. Do not ignore the calls.
- The police must provide interpretation in a language you understand. Avoid using a friend or family member as your interpreter.
- The statement record is written in Korean. Ask for it to be read back to you through the interpreter before you sign, and have anything wrong corrected.
- If you are arrested, you can ask for your consulate to be notified.

The outcome of a criminal case can also affect your next visa extension, so think about your stay from the start.
